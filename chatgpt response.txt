## 2. First-principles assessment of the idea

Start with the actual user problem rather than the AI technology.

A person receives what looks like a legitimate job offer. A recruiter says the candidate has been selected, but they must pay a registration fee, security deposit, document-verification charge, or some other upfront amount. The person may not know how to distinguish an official hiring process from an impersonation scam.

The user's real question is not simply, "Does this company exist?"

It is:

"Is this specific offer and the person contacting me trustworthy enough for me to proceed?"

That is the problem NaukriShield should solve.

### The critical weakness I found during research

There are already adjacent services doing parts of this job.

- The Indian government's National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal has a suspect repository for checking identifiers such as mobile numbers, email addresses, account numbers and URLs. It also provides a way to report suspect identifiers.&#x20;

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.cybercrime.gov.in\&sz=32)

  Cyber Crime Portal

- ScamRadar currently advertises an AI-powered scam checker for WhatsApp messages, screenshots, job offers and UPI-related scams, with Hindi-English results and recommended actions. That is remarkably close to NaukriShield's currently stated proposition. I have verified its public product claims, not its detection accuracy or adoption.&#x20;

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.scamradar.in\&sz=32)

  ScamRadar



This does not mean you should abandon NaukriShield. It means that "upload a screenshot and get a Hindi scam verdict" is not enough of a differentiator.

The better positioning is:

> NaukriShield: an evidence-backed job-offer investigation agent that checks the offer, not just the company.

The key difference should be that NaukriShield investigates, explains what it could verify, finds contradictions, changes its next step when evidence is missing, and produces a reviewable evidence report rather than simply generating a red warning badge.

That gives you a stronger product story and, more importantly, a stronger way to demonstrate agent behavior.

## 3. The five weaknesses in Claude Code's current plan

Weakness 1 — Four agents may be unnecessary complexity

The proposed Intake, Verifier, Risk and Action agents sound impressive on a diagram. But four independent agents introduce coordination overhead, more failure points and a larger debugging surface. The judging criterion is an agent that completes a substantial task, not the number of agents you create.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://neatlogs.com\&sz=32)

neatlogs



Change: Build one stateful orchestrator with a small set of real tools. It should decide what to check based on the information it has, continue when one source fails, and verify the final result. Split components into separate agents only when a genuine technical reason emerges.

Weakness 2 — Crustdata cannot prove that a job offer is genuine

Crustdata provides company and people data, including company discovery and professional-profile information. That can help establish whether an employer or recruiter has a corresponding public profile, but it is not a dedicated job-fraud or UPI-fraud database.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.crustdata.com\&sz=32)

Crustdata

+2



A real company can be impersonated by a scammer. A missing profile can also mean incomplete data rather than fraud.

Change: Treat Crustdata as one source of evidence. Check the correspondence between the claimed employer, sender domain, recruiter identity and offer details. Clearly separate verified facts from missing information.

Weakness 3 — XGBoost is not justified yet

The proposed six-feature XGBoost model may add complexity without improving the product. The chat does not establish a suitable, evaluated dataset of Indian job-offer scams from which you could reliably train a model within the deadline.

Change: Start with explicit, explainable checks: requests for upfront payments, mismatched domains, contradictory company or recruiter information, suspicious links, and pressure tactics. A model is optional only if you can demonstrate that it adds measurable value. A rules engine that works is better than an unvalidated ML model.

Weakness 4 — The proposed stack is too broad for the time remaining

Streamlit, FastAPI, XGBoost, SQLite, Qdrant, Convex, LiteLLM, OCR and multiple agents create too many independent things to debug. You do not need two web frameworks or multiple state stores to prove this product works.

Change: Use Streamlit, Python, SQLite if persistence is needed, the provided model access, Crustdata, and neatlogs. Make OCR optional if its dependency becomes a blocker. Do not add Qdrant, Convex or a separate FastAPI server unless the working MVP proves they are necessary.

Weakness 5 — The architecture does not yet demonstrate the mandatory sponsor tools

Your summarized chat proposes an application architecture, but it does not document a completed before-and-after improvement, an actual recovery run, an Entire checkpoint or repository analysis, or a cfo.ai business plan.

These are not decorative extras. The official rules require neatlogs, Entire and cfo.ai and explicitly require the video to cover their architecture, implementation, runtime, reasoning and results. The demo must be no longer than three minutes.&#x20;

## 4. The version I would actually build to compete

Keep the name NaukriShield, but make its core feature an evidence-backed investigation rather than a simple scam classifier.

THE PRODUCT PROMISE

# Verify the offer, not just the company.

A user pastes a job message or uploads a screenshot. NaukriShield investigates the claim, explains the evidence in Hindi or English, and recommends the next safe action.

1\. Extract the facts

Identify the company, recruiter, sender email or domain, links, promised salary, payment demands and any urgency tactics. Support pasted text first; add screenshot OCR if it works reliably.

2\. Investigate with tools

Use Crustdata to look up available company and recruiter information. Check domain consistency and the offer's stated conditions. Where feasible, direct the user to the government's suspect repository for relevant identifiers.

3\. Choose the next step from the evidence

If the company is identified but the sender domain is inconsistent, investigate that inconsistency. If a lookup fails, retry once and use a fallback rather than silently treating missing data as proof of fraud.

4\. Verify the result and explain it

Return evidence, sources, contradictions, missing checks, an appropriately cautious risk assessment, and a Hindi or English explanation. Generate a complaint draft when appropriate; never submit it automatically.

### What makes this an agent rather than an ordinary chatbot?

The system must demonstrate that the result of one action changes what it does next.

A fixed sequence of four functions followed by an LLM-generated explanation will not convincingly demonstrate that. A stateful investigation loop that selects checks, records evidence, handles failures and verifies its conclusion is a stronger fit for the hackathon's actual objective.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://neatlogs.com\&sz=32)

For the demo, build two clearly labeled test cases: a suspicious offer that asks for an upfront payment through a mismatched domain, and a contrasting offer with fewer obvious warning signs. Use fictionalized examples or clearly identified test fixtures; do not falsely accuse a real company of fraud.

Also, never equate "no red flags found" with "safe." Your output should distinguish high risk, needs verification, and no strong red flags found in the checks performed.

If money has already been transferred, the app should direct the victim toward the official Indian cybercrime reporting process and the 1930 helpline for financial cyber fraud.&#x20;

## 1. Product direction

Build **NaukriShield: an evidence-backed job-offer investigation agent for Indian job seekers.**

Core promise: "Verify the offer, not just the company."

A user pastes a suspicious WhatsApp, Telegram or email job offer, or uploads a screenshot if OCR works reliably. NaukriShield investigates the offer, explains its findings in Hindi or English, and recommends safe next steps.

## 2. Simplify the architecture

Use a single stateful Python orchestrator with a bounded investigation loop, not four independent LLM agents just to make the system look sophisticated.

Implement the smallest reliable flow:

1. Extract structured details from the job offer.
2. Choose which verification checks are relevant.
3. Use Crustdata's company and people APIs where appropriate.
4. Check sender-domain consistency and explicit scam indicators such as demands for upfront payments.
5. Adapt the next action based on the retrieved evidence.
6. Retry a failed tool at most once, then use a fallback or report that the information could not be verified.
7. Verify the evidence supporting the final assessment.
8. Produce a Hindi/English report, evidence list and complaint draft when appropriate.

Every material risk claim must be linked to an observation or source. Never treat a missing Crustdata profile as proof of fraud, and never treat the existence of a real company as proof that a particular job offer is genuine.

Use cautious assessment categories such as HIGH RISK, NEEDS VERIFICATION, and NO STRONG RED FLAGS FOUND IN CHECKS PERFORMED. Do not claim guaranteed accuracy.

Keep screenshot OCR optional if its installation or reliability threatens the MVP. Pasted text must work end to end. Do not add XGBoost, Qdrant, Convex, FastAPI or another service unless a demonstrated requirement makes it necessary.
